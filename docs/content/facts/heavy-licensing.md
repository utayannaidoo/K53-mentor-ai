# Fact base — PrDP, the heavy-vehicle driving test, and Code EB towing

Verified 27 November 2026 for the guides `prdp-professional-driving-permit`,
`code-10-code-14-driving-test` and `code-eb-trailer-licence`. The Act and its regulations
outrank everything else here. The text read is the Acts Online consolidation of the National
Road Traffic Regulations, 2000 (R.225), not the Government Gazette; its amendment notes run to
about 2016, so a later amendment could change a figure.

## Old codes and new codes

- The regulations use the codes **B, C1, C, EB, EC1, EC** [REG 99(4)(a)]. Code 8, 10, 11, 13
  and 14 are the pre-1998 names everyone still uses. The guides keep the established mapping
  (Code 8 = B, Code 10 = C1, Code 14 = EC) already used by `which-licence-code-8-10-14`. The
  conversion table in reg 99(4)(b) is published as images on Acts Online and wasn't read.
- **C1**: rigid, tare (or GVM for a bus or goods vehicle) over 3 500 kg and not over 16 000 kg,
  with or without a trailer of GVM not over 750 kg. Includes B [REG 99(4)(a)].
- **C**: rigid bus or goods vehicle with GVM over 16 000 kg, trailer up to 750 kg. Includes B and
  C1 [REG 99(4)(a)].
- **EC1**: articulated (truck-tractor GCM over 3 500 and not over 16 000 kg), or a C1-size
  vehicle drawing a trailer over 750 kg GVM. Includes B, C1 and EB [REG 99(4)(a)].
- **EC**: articulated (truck-tractor GCM over 16 000 kg), or a bus or goods vehicle over
  16 000 kg GVM drawing a trailer over 750 kg. Includes B, C1, C, EB and EC1 [REG 99(4)(a)].
- The heavy learner's licence is **Code 3**: any motor vehicle or combination other than a
  motorcycle, tricycle or quadricycle [REG 99(1)(c)]. The light one is **Code 2**: up to
  3 500 kg tare, including combinations whose drawing vehicle is up to 3 500 kg [REG 99(1)(b)].
- **No prerequisite code**: to take the driving test you need a learner's licence that covers
  the class you are applying for [REG 107(2)(a)]. Nothing read requires Code 10 before Code 14.
- Ages: gov.za gives **18** for C1, EC1, B and EB, and 17 for A1 [GOV-DL]. It gives no age for
  C or EC; Act s15(1)(a)(iii) disqualifies anyone **under 18** from any licence not listed
  lower, so 18 is the floor. Say "18", not "18 for every heavy code by gov.za".
- Learner's licence validity: Acts Online's reg 101(1) reads 18 months, but gov.za says
  **24 months** (`licensing.md`, [GOV-LL]). The consolidation predates the change; **use 24**.

## Professional driving permit (PrDP)

- **Who needs one** [REG 115(1)]: the driver of (a) a goods vehicle with GVM over **3 500 kg**;
  (b) a breakdown vehicle; (c) a bus; (d) a minibus over 3 500 kg GVM or built for **12 or more
  persons including the driver**; (e) a motor vehicle carrying **persons for reward**, or
  operated under an operating licence; (f) a vehicle over 3 500 kg GVM carrying dangerous goods
  under regs 273–283 (the reg says "to which regulations 273 to 283 apply" — those are the
  dangerous-goods regulations; describe it as dangerous goods); (g) any motor vehicle conveying
  12 or more persons including the driver.
- **Exempt** [REG 115(2)]: traffic officers and examiners on duty; a **hearse**; a **tractor**;
  vehicles under reg 21(1)/(5); and a **learner** driving that vehicle while **accompanied by
  a holder of a valid PrDP** for it.
- **Categories** [REG 116(1)]:
  - **G** — goods: items (a) and (b), goods vehicles over 3 500 kg and breakdown vehicles.
  - **P** — passengers: (a), (b), (c), (d), (e) and (g). So P also covers goods.
  - **D** — dangerous goods: all of 115(1). D is the widest; it covers what G and P cover.
- A PrDP only works for a vehicle the holder also has a **valid driving licence** for
  [REG 116(3)]. A Code B holder with a P permit can carry passengers for reward in a
  Code B vehicle, not drive a bus.
- **Ages** [REG 117(aA)]: **21** for P, **25** for D. The regulation sets no separate age for G;
  in practice the floor is the licence it needs (18 for C1).
- **Disqualified** [REG 117]: no valid driving licence for the vehicle; not certified medically
  fit on **form MC** by a registered medical or occupational health practitioner **within
  2 months** before applying; in the **5 years** before applying, convicted of (or paid an
  admission of guilt for) driving under the influence, driving over the alcohol limit, or
  **reckless driving** — and for P and D, any offence with **violence** as an element; or
  while a licence or PrDP is suspended or cancelled.
- **Dangerous-goods training**: from a date the Minister sets in the Gazette, a D applicant needs
  a certificate from an approved training body obtained **within 6 months** before applying
  [REG 117(e)]. Whether that date has been set wasn't confirmed, so the guide says D applicants
  should expect to need the course and check with the centre. **Don't state it as in force.**
- **Applying** [REG 118]: at a **driving licence testing centre** on **form PD1**, with the
  provincial fee, identification, **two photographs**, the **original driving licence**, the
  **form MC** medical certificate, any training certificate, and any PrDP already held. The
  centre asks the nearest **SAPS station** for a report of the convictions above [REG 118(4)].
  It is not called a police clearance certificate in the regulation.
- **Valid 24 months** from authorisation [REG 122(1)]. Apply **on or before the expiry date** and
  the new permit runs 24 months from the day after the old one expires. The regulation gives
  **no grace period** after expiry: an expired PrDP means not driving that vehicle.
- Alcohol: a professional driver's limit is 0,02 g per 100 ml of blood (`rules.md`).

## The heavy-vehicle driving test (codes C1, C, EC1, EC)

- **Manual**: Volume 2, Heavy motor vehicles [REG 107(5)(b); K53 V2]. Code B uses Volume 1.
- **Test vehicle**: a roadworthy heavy vehicle or combination **with a seat next to the
  driver**, supplied by the applicant; **no load is required** [K53 V2 §2.1]. The volume covers
  vehicles over 3 500 kg and a minimum total length of **6 m** [K53 V2 §1.5]. An articulated
  vehicle or combination is tested **coupled** — semi-trailer on the truck-tractor, trailer on the
  drawing vehicle [REG 107(3)].
- **Yard manoeuvres** [K53 V2 §2.2], in any order: **alley docking to the right**, **reversing
  in a straight line**, **the left turn**, and **the incline start**. Code B's yard is the turn
  in the road, alley docking left and right, parallel parking left and right, and the incline
  start [K53 V1 §2.2]. So heavy drops parallel parking and the turn in the road, adds the
  straight reverse and the left turn, and docks on the right only.
- Module numbers: left turn 15, straight reverse 16, alley docking right 17, incline start 18
  (manual) or 19 (automatic) [K53 V2 §3, modules].
- The yard test is timed at **20 minutes** [K53 V2 §3].
- **Road test**: must include a **lane change and at least one hand signal** [K53 V2 §3.2.3].
  There is **no emergency stop** — Volume 1 has one (§3.2.5), Volume 2 doesn't.
- **Pre-trip inspection** additions for heavy vehicles [K53 V2 modules 1–2]: done with the
  **engine idling**, partly to find leaks in the braking system; check for **air leaks**; check
  **air tanks** for damage and stability and test for **water in the tanks** where applicable;
  on an articulated vehicle check **service lines and couplings** and the **fifth-wheel
  coupling with its safety locking pin**; check the **chevron** and **information plate**
  (and the trailer's); the **operator card** and roadworthy/clearance disc; inside, warning
  lights and gauges and pressure on the brake and clutch pedals. The volume names the checks,
  not pressure figures — **publish no air-pressure numbers**.

## Code EB and towing

- **Code B tows a trailer with GVM up to 750 kg.** A light vehicle (tare up to 3 500 kg, or a
  bus or goods vehicle up to 3 500 kg GVM) drawing a trailer **over 750 kg GVM** needs **EB**,
  as does an articulated vehicle whose truck-tractor GCM is up to 3 500 kg [REG 99(4)(a)]. EB
  includes B.
- It's the trailer's **GVM** — its rated maximum laden mass — that counts, not what is loaded on
  it today. A trailer rated over 750 kg needs EB even when empty. (Follows from the regulation
  using GVM; GVM is the manufacturer's maximum mass for the vehicle with its load, Act s1.)
- **No EB learner's**: the learner's licence for a light combination is **Code 2** [REG 99(1)(b)].
- **Age 18** [GOV-DL].
- **The test**: Volume 4, *Light motor vehicles combination* [REG 107(5)(d)], with the **trailer
  attached** [REG 107(3)]. The content of Volume 4 wasn't read — **say nothing about its
  manoeuvres**.
- **Trailer brakes** [REG 151(1)], by the trailer's GVM against the towing vehicle's tare:
  - **Up to 750 kg**: if the trailer's GVM is up to **half** the towing vehicle's tare, no service
    brake, but it needs a **parking brake** or another way to hold it stationary. Over half,
    up to the tare: parking brake plus an **overrun** or service brake. Over the tare: parking
    brake plus a **service brake**.
  - **Over 750 kg up to 3 500 kg**: up to the towing vehicle's tare, parking brake plus overrun or
    service brake; over the tare, parking brake plus service brake.
  - **Over 3 500 kg**: parking brake plus service brake.
  - A trailer's service brake must be operable by the driver of the towing vehicle while
    moving [REG 151(2)].
- No regulation read caps the mass a light vehicle may tow; the **manufacturer's towing rating**
  is the limit to check. Don't invent a "you may tow X times your tare" rule.
- **Combinations** [REG 222(1)]: a drawing vehicle with **one or two trailers**. Towing a
  broken-down vehicle plus a trailer is allowed only in an emergency or breakdown.
- **Towing a vehicle** [REG 330]: connection no longer than **3,5 m**; the towed vehicle's
  steering controlled by a licence holder for it; above **30 km/h** only with a **tow-bar or
  drawbar**; the towed vehicle's brakes must meet reg 155 unless a tow-bar or drawbar is used;
  no motorcycle, tricycle, quadricycle or pedal cycle may tow.
  - **Passengers in a towed vehicle**: reg 330(f) bars carrying persons in it **above 30 km/h**,
    unless it's a semi-trailer. Motus p.65 teaches "no passengers in a towed vehicle" outright,
    and that's the answer the learner's bank expects. Guides give the manual's rule and note
    the regulation's wording.
- **Speed**: no trailer-specific limit for a light combination; the general limits apply
  [REG 292]. **80 km/h** applies to goods vehicles, goods-drawn combinations and articulated
  vehicles over **9 000 kg** and to any breakdown vehicle towing another vehicle [REG 293].
